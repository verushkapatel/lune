The mission

Most adults learn money by getting it wrong. For a student from a low-income family, adulthood begins right after Grade 12, with no university years to learn in and no one at home who has done it before. One mistake can cost months of savings. So we teach the lessons two years early, in a classroom, where a wrong answer costs nothing.

0% of Indian adults are financially literate, against 52% in advanced economies

How it works

Find the gap.
Then fill it.

Four steps, from a fifteen-question test to a habit that lasts.

  1. 01 · Test

    Fifteen real situations.

    The Money Readiness Test, adapted from the S&P Global Financial Literacy Survey and rewritten for a sixteen-year-old. Every wrong option is a real money mistake.

    Try one →

    Question 1 · Warm up

    Meera has ₹800 this month. She can't afford everything. What should she pay for first?

  2. 02 · AI

    A model reads every answer.

    Results go through our AI model, topic by topic, student by student. It finds where the class is weakest, so no minute of teaching goes where it isn't needed.

    Illustrative class · 32 students × 6 topics

  3. 03 · Teach

    Two hours, aimed at the gap.

    Each student sees their own score privately. Then an 80-minute course on earning, borrowing and staying safe, with most of the time on what the class found hardest.

    Day 2 · 120 minutes

    • 04 · Saath

      Then they live it.

      Two sessions are easy to forget. Saath turns the lesson into practice: every student leaves with a pocket handbook and an app where every choice changes the money.

    Field notes · The first pilot

    What 102 students
    taught us.

    August 2026 · Pune & Mumbai

    102 students in Grades 9 to 12 sat our pilot paper.

    88 of them travel to school by bus or private car.

    91 own a laptop or tablet.

    These were well-resourced students.

    They scored 81% on careful reading and everyday sums.

    And 49% on how money behaves outside a classroom.

    73 scored 80% or better across Parts A to C.

    30 of those same students scored half or less on Part D.

    Reading a question closely is a school skill. These students have it.

    If they sit at 49%, the gap for students with fewer advantages is wider.

    That is the gap we exist to close.
