Listen

Real piano sound from the score. Play the whole piece, scrub the timeline to jump anywhere, and the playhead stays with you.

Letters or fingers

One clear layer. Never both.

Toggle letter names or finger numbers — exclusive, so the staff stays readable. Chords stack every tone in pitch order. Dense pages thin singles carefully; triad tones are never dropped.

Digital piano

Watch the keys press with you.

An on-screen keyboard mirrors playback — chords light together, finger digits appear on pressed keys, and speed runs from slow-mo to brisk so hard bars can be learned under the hands.

Ask about a bar

Click the measure that will not sit.

Click a bar on the score and get letter names, fingerings, and short practice notes for that measure — right beside the page.

Why Lune exists

Built for the hours alone at the piano.

I’m Verushka Patel — fifteen, an ABRSM Grade 7 pianist. Playing by ear never came easily, so sight reading felt like the hardest part of every new piece. On harder music, figuring out which finger belonged where was frustrating enough that practice stalled.

I made Lune so that loop could feel calmer: meet the score, hear it, see letter names or finger numbers clearly, and ask about the bars that won’t sit under the hands yet.

Search any public-domain title above, or upload your own MusicXML, PDF, or photo. Black and white on purpose — the music is the point.

Credits & licenses

Lune is a free, non-commercial practice tool. It stands on other people’s generous work:

Scores

  • Humdrum editions by Craig Stuart Sapp (Beethoven, Chopin, Haydn, Joplin, Mozart, Scarlatti, Scriabin). Several are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: credit the editor, non-commercial use, share alike.
  • Humdrum encodings of Bach by David Huron, and Bach chorales from the music21 corpus.
  • OpenScore (CC0) — Für Elise.
  • The MuseTrainer public-domain MusicXML library (Chopin nocturnes, Debussy, Liszt, Satie, Bach minuet).
  • MuseScore community uploads (Mozart K. 545, Clementi Sonatinas) — linked on each piece.

Each piece shows its own source and license under the score and the overview. All compositions are in the public domain; if you hold rights in an edition here and want it removed, open an issue and it will be taken down.

Words

  • Composer portraits are public-domain images from Wikimedia Commons.

Software & type

  • OpenSheetMusicDisplay (BSD-3-Clause) with VexFlow (MIT) · Tone.js (MIT) · pdf.js (Apache-2.0) · Fraunces typeface (SIL Open Font License 1.1).

Privacy

  • No accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no ads.
  • Scores you upload are read inside your browser. They are never sent anywhere and are gone when you close the tab.
  • When you press play, piano sounds load from tonejs.github.io; GitHub Pages hosts the site. Neither receives anything about your scores.
