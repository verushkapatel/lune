Try it for yourself

Open the real studio on Clair de lune — or sign in first so your plans and notes stay with you. Signing up is free.

Reading

Dyslexia-friendly letters

On Overview or Score, tap Aa. Letter names switch to Atkinson Hyperlegible — clearer shapes, less mix-up between similar letters. Tap again to turn it off. You can also open Reading & access for large print and high contrast.

Braille

Braille music downloads

When a library piece has a braille file, a Braille chip appears on Overview or Score. Download the .brf for a refreshable display or embosser. Not every score has one yet — Clair de lune and several others do.

In the studio

One quiet toolkit.

  • Read Letter names or fingers under the staff. Dyslexia-friendly type and braille when you need them.
  • Hear Play with a metronome in the piece’s meter, and a tempo slider marked at the original bpm.
  • See the keys Falling-note piano tutorial — notes drop onto the keyboard, finger on the key.
  • Your practice plan Select bars, speak or jot a note — Lune summarises a plan into your Repertoire.

Every note, named.

Letters or finger numbers sit in their own lane under the staff, never on the notes. Toggle one or the other so the page stays readable.

Hear it, see the keys.

Play the score at any speed and the playhead stays with you. An on-screen keyboard lights the keys as they sound, so hard bars can be learned under the hands.

Tap the bar that won’t sit.

Click a bar and the coach lists its notes, suggested fingers, and how to practise it. One panel, beside the page.

Practise what you’d forget.

Write or speak a note on a bar, then rate it after you practise. Lune brings the hard ones back as Today’s bars, so you work what you would forget.

Why Lune exists

Built for the hours alone at the piano.

I’m Verushka Patel, fifteen, an ABRSM Grade 7 pianist. Sight reading and fingering used to stall my practice. I made Lune so those hours alone at the piano could feel calmer: find a score, hear it, see letter names or finger numbers, and work the bars that will not sit yet.

Privacy

  • No cookies, no analytics, no ads. Lune never sells or shares anything about you.
  • Your Repertoire, bar notes and review dates are saved in your own browser. If you choose to make an account, they’re stored in Lune’s database (Supabase) under your email address, locked so only you can read them. You can download or delete everything from the Account panel at any time.
  • Scores you upload are read inside your browser. They leave your device only if you add them to your Repertoire while signed in, and then only into your private storage.
  • The microphone (voice notes, Play along) is used only while you ask Lune to listen. Audio is analysed in the browser and never recorded or uploaded; voice notes may use your browser’s built-in speech recognition, which can be provided by its maker (for example Google in Chrome, Apple in Safari).
  • Under 13? Ask a parent or guardian before making an account.
  • The reading study records only a participant code, answers and timings — never names.
  • When you press play, piano sounds load from tonejs.github.io; the site is hosted at lune.page.
