1 · Understand a bar

Tap a bar. Ask what is going on.

What’s happening in bar 12?

The right hand plays C5 with finger 3, then E4, D5 and C5 with fingers 1, 4 and 2. The left hand plays A2, E3, A3 with 5, 2, 1. The hard part is the right hand’s leap up a seventh, from E4 to D5. Find D5 with your eyes before the thumb leaves E4.

On any score: tap a bar, then Ask Lune.

2 · A plan for the time you have

Say how long you have. Get the steps.

I have 20 minutes tonight. What should I do?

1. Bar 12, right hand alone: the E4 to D5 leap silently, then with sound. 5 minutes.
2. Bars 26 and 30, hands separately. 7 minutes.
3. Bars 1 to 30 together at 60 bpm, then 66. 8 minutes.

Saved to Repertoire with a one-line summary, so tomorrow starts where tonight stopped.

3 · When a bar falls apart

Rate it Hard. Get one thing to try.

Bar 26 · Hard

The left hand keeps returning to B flat while the right hand jumps from D5 up to B flat 5. Say “B flat” aloud as you play the left hand alone twice, then join the hands at half speed.

Hard and Again bars also come back sooner in Today’s bars.

4 · Speak, and it is written down

Talk while your hands are busy.

● “bar thirty keep the thumb light on the A”

Noted on bar 30: “Keep the thumb light on the A.” It is flagged on the score.

Whisper writes down what you say, with punctuation. Answers can be read back in a natural voice.

5 · Chat with Lune

Ask about practice, not just one bar.

How do I stop rushing the semiquavers?

This is general advice, not from your score. Set the metronome a little under your comfortable speed and count the semiquavers aloud for one bar. Let the first note of each beat land on the click, and go faster only after three clean runs.

From Home or Settings. It knows your Repertoire, your plans and your goal.

The maker

Built by a teenager, for the hours alone at the piano.

I’m Verushka Patel, sixteen, an ABRSM Grade 7 pianist. Sight reading and fingering used to stall my practice. I made Lune so those hours alone at the piano could feel calmer: find a score, hear it, see letter names or finger numbers, and work the bars that will not sit yet.

The name comes from Debussy’s Clair de lune — the piece I put on whenever I feel lost. Somehow everything settles again. I hoped this app could do a little of the same for practice: a quiet place that helps you find your way back to the music.

Privacy

  • No cookies, no analytics, no ads. Lune never sells or shares anything about you.
  • Your Repertoire, bar notes and review dates are saved in your own browser. If you choose to make an account, they’re stored in Lune’s database (Supabase) under your email address, locked so only you can read them. You can download or delete everything from the Account panel at any time.
  • Scores you upload are read inside your browser. They leave your device only if you add them to your Repertoire while signed in, and then only into your private storage.
  • The microphone (Ask Lune, voice remarks) is used only while you ask Lune to listen. Audio is analysed in the browser and never recorded or uploaded; voice notes may use your browser’s built-in speech recognition, which can be provided by its maker (for example Google in Chrome, Apple in Safari).
  • Under 13? Ask a parent or guardian before making an account.
  • Lune AI (for people with an account): when you ask it something, your question, the score facts for that bar or piece, and your remarks, ratings and plans for that piece are sent to Lune’s server on Cloudflare, which passes them to the model (run by Cloudflare Workers AI) and sends back the answer. Lune’s server does not store or log them; Cloudflare describes its own handling in its Workers AI terms. Your questions, answers and chats are saved only in this browser. Signed out, Lune AI is off.
  • Ask Lune’s built-in answers are worked out on your device and nothing you ask is sent anywhere. If you connect a local model in Settings (for example Ollama on your own computer), your question, the score facts for that bar, your remarks, ratings and plans for that piece go to the address you entered and nowhere else. With a model on your own computer, that address is your own machine. If you ever enter an address on another computer, that data is sent there.
  • Remarks, ratings, plans and your Repertoire stay in this browser unless you sign in; signed in, they are stored under your account. A share only ever contains the text shown in “What will be shared”, which you can edit first.
  • The reading study records only a participant code, answers and timings — never names.
  • Feedback you send from the Feedback form is emailed to Lune’s maker through FormSubmit (formsubmit.co) and may be kept in Lune’s database. It holds only what you type and the choices you tick; the form never asks for your email address.
  • When you press play, piano sounds load from tonejs.github.io; the site is hosted at lune.page.
