Inside the studio

Each tool, as you’ll use it.

No slideshow. Scroll through the real studio — one feature at a time, with the why beside it.

01 · Annotate

Every note, named.

Why: Labels sit under the staff in their own lane — never painted on the note heads — so the page stays readable while you learn.

Toggle Notes, Fingers, or Off in the score tools. One mode at a time.

02 · Hear

Your tempo. The original marked.

Why: Slowing down shouldn’t lose the piece’s pulse. The slider keeps the original bpm visible so you always know where “real” sits.

Play with a metronome in the piece’s meter. The playhead stays with you on the page.

03 · Piano

Falling notes onto real keys.

Why: Hard bars make more sense under the hands. Same tempo control as the score — filter right, left, or both.

Open the Piano tab. Notes drop onto the keyboard; pressed keys can show the finger digit.

04 · Play along

Lune listens while you play.

Why: Wrong notes and hesitations should become a map you can practise from — not a vague feeling that something went wrong.

Tap Play along under the score. Lune uses the microphone in the browser only, marks the bars that trip you, then offers tonight’s short plan. Nothing is recorded or uploaded.

05 · Ask

Tap the bar that won’t sit.

Why: Help should open beside the page you tapped — notes, suggested fingers, and a quiet way to practise — not a wall of chrome.

Click a stubborn bar. The coach panel lists what’s in it and how to work it.

06 · Easy read

Letters that refuse to blur.

Why: Similar note names mix up under fatigue. Atkinson Hyperlegible keeps every letter shape distinct under the staff.

Tap Aa on Score or Overview. Large print and high contrast live in Reading & access.

07 · Braille

Music you can feel.

Why: When a library piece has a braille file, download should be one quiet chip away — for an embosser or refreshable display.

Not every score has one yet. Clair de lune and several others do.

08 · Notes

Speak or jot what you’d forget.

Why: Practice thoughts vanish between sessions. Put them on the bar — typed or spoken — and rate it after you work it.

Markers sit on the score. Hard bars come back as Today’s work.

09 · Repertoire

Plans that stay with you.

Why: Select bars, speak or jot a note, save a plan into Repertoire. Sign in and it syncs — no ads, no payments.

Today’s bars bring back what you’d forget. Clair de lune opens after you make Lune yours.

Why Lune exists

Named for
Clair de lune.

The maker

Built by a teenager,
for the hours alone
at the piano.

I’m Verushka Patel, fifteen, an ABRSM Grade 7 pianist. Sight reading and fingering used to stall my practice. I made Lune so those hours alone at the piano could feel calmer: find a score, hear it, see letter names or finger numbers, and work the bars that will not sit yet.

The name comes from Debussy’s Clair de lune — the piece I put on whenever I feel lost. Somehow everything settles again. I hoped this app could do a little of the same for practice: a quiet place that helps you find your way back to the music.

Free forever

Make Lune
yours.

A short questionnaire — composers, level, dream piece — then an email code. Your path stays with you.

Credits & licenses

Lune is a free, non-commercial practice tool. It stands on other people’s generous work:

Scores

  • Humdrum editions by Craig Stuart Sapp (Beethoven, Chopin, Haydn, Joplin, Mozart, Scarlatti, Scriabin). Several are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: credit the editor, non-commercial use, share alike.
  • Humdrum encodings of Bach by David Huron, and Bach chorales from the music21 corpus.
  • OpenScore (CC0) — Für Elise.
  • The MuseTrainer public-domain MusicXML library (Chopin nocturnes, Debussy, Liszt, Satie, Bach minuet).
  • MuseScore community uploads (Mozart K. 545, Clementi Sonatinas) — linked on each piece.

Each piece shows its own source and license under the score and the overview. All compositions are in the public domain; if you hold rights in an edition here and want it removed, open an issue and it will be taken down.

Privacy

  • No cookies, no analytics, no ads. Lune never sells or shares anything about you.
  • Your Repertoire, bar notes and review dates are saved in your own browser. If you choose to make an account, they’re stored in Lune’s database (Supabase) under your email address, locked so only you can read them. You can download or delete everything from the Account panel at any time.
  • Scores you upload are read inside your browser. They leave your device only if you add them to your Repertoire while signed in, and then only into your private storage.
  • The microphone (voice notes, Play along) is used only while you ask Lune to listen. Audio is analysed in the browser and never recorded or uploaded; voice notes may use your browser’s built-in speech recognition, which can be provided by its maker (for example Google in Chrome, Apple in Safari).
  • Under 13? Ask a parent or guardian before making an account.
  • The reading study records only a participant code, answers and timings — never names.
  • When you press play, piano sounds load from tonejs.github.io; the site is hosted at lune.page.
